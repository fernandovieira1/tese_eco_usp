Existing approaches illuminate parts of these relationships, but their joint theoretical implications for production, incentives, and environmental governance remain insufficiently developed.

As we will argue, the spatial dimension is especially important. Carbon compensation programs may be fungible for the purpose of accounting for a global atmospheric stock, but equivalent quantities of carbon do not imply equivalence in biodiversity, hydrological regulation, soil resilience, or ecological connectivity. Compensation undertaken elsewhere may satisfy a market or regulatory requirement without restoring the local ecological functions that sustain a particular productive territory. Likewise, adding an environmental variable $E$ to a production function does not by itself make the environment endogenous: endogeneity requires that $E$ affect productive outcomes while its condition also responds, directly or indirectly, to production, preservation, institutional rules, and ecological feedback.

%% Contribuições
The article aims to make three principal contributions. First, to propose a more precise representation of nature in production theory by distinguishing the mere inclusion of a natural-resource input from the endogeneity of an ecological state that affects productivity and responds to economic decisions. Second, to connect this representation to private incentives and ecologically grounded governance, emphasizing that environmental equivalence at the level of carbon accounting does not necessarily preserve locally specific ecosystem functions. Third, it introduces economic commensalism as a testable analytical framework and derives theoretical conditions for distinguishing productive use that maintains ecological functionality from resource-use regimes that erode the basis of future production. The proposed framework does not reject regulation, enforcement, markets, or collective governance; it reorganizes their roles around the problem of making preservation a more robust component of the agent's rational decision rather than an exclusively external burden.
