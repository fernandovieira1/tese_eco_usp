# Critical review and source audit — Section 2

## Editorial decisions

The revised section adopts the same central qualification as the Introduction: the gap is one of analytical integration, not the absence of environmental thought, policy, or institutions. The argument therefore avoids three claims that would be difficult to defend under peer review:

1. the continued existence of degradation is not treated as proof that every existing instrument has failed;
2. private rationality, social efficiency, ecological sustainability, and legal compliance are treated as distinct evaluative categories;
3. economic commensalism is presented as the framework motivated by a residual disconnect, not as an already validated or universally superior solution.

The section also separates international agreements, command-and-control regulation, carbon pricing, payments for environmental services, financial instruments, and collaborative governance. Their limitations are stated in terms of scope, implementation conditions, policy interaction, and institutional fit. Canonical theoretical discussions of Pigou, Coase, Hardin, Ostrom, and natural capital remain reserved for Section 3, in accordance with the article's roadmap.

The rough-draft citations to Easterly, Lomborg, and Agrawal were not retained because the cited works do not directly substantiate the specific institutional or empirical propositions to which they had been attached. This is a relevance judgment, not a claim that the publications do not exist.
