Data structure and naming convention for LR data 
The LR directory contains data on different topics organized by folder: 
• aez – with results by climate and time period on AEZ classification by 
climate/soil/terrain/LC (33 or 57 classes), moisture regime class and thermal regime 
classes  
• excl – exclusion class layer 
• lco – land cover class layers 
• socio – selected socio-economic and demographic data 
• soi1 – soil resources layers 
• soi2 – soil Suitability 
• ter – terrain resources layers 
• wat – water resources layers 
Please refer to Annex I for a complete list variables. 
Data structure and naming convention for res01 data 
The res01 directory contains: 
• Historical observational climate data 
• Modelled climate projections from multiple Global Climate Models (GCMs) 
• Annual time-series datasets (TS) for each data category 
Historical observational data are organized within the CRUTS32/Hist directory and include annual 
time-series datasets stored in the TS folder. 
CRUTS32/ 
└── Hist/ 
    └── TS/        # Annual time-series datasets 
Historical time-series results include three 30-year average periods (6190 = 1961–1990; 7100 = 
1971–2000; 8110 = 1981–2010), one 10-year average period (0010 = 2000–2010) and annual 
time-series (TS) datasets from 1960 to 2010. 
Projected modelled data are organized by climate model (five folders) and Representative 
Concentration Pathways (RCPs) (four folders). In addition to individual model outputs, results for 
an ENSEMBLE mean scenario are also provided. The assessment includes the following climate 
models derived from recent IPCC AR5 Earth System Model (ESM) outputs: 
• GFDL-ESM2M 
• HadGEM2-ES 
• IPSL-CM5A-LR 
• MIROC-ESM-CHEM 
• NorESM1-M 
 
 
Model Description 
GFDL-ESM2M The Geophysical Fluid Dynamics Laboratory (GFDL) has constructed 
NOAA’s first Earth System Models (ESMs) to advance our understanding 
of how the Earth's biogeochemical cycles, including human actions, 
interact with the climate system. 
HadGEM2-ES It is a coupled Earth System Model that was used by the Met Office 
Hadley Centre for the CMIP5 centennial simulations. 
IPSL-CM5A-LR It is developed by IPSL, includes 5 component models representing the 
Earth System climate and its carbon cycle: LMDz (atmosphere), NEMO 
(ocean, oceanic biogeochemistry and sea-ice), ORCHIDEE (continental 
surfaces and vegetation), and INCA (atmospheric chemistry), coupled 
through OASIS. 
MIROC-ESM-CHEM It is based on a global climate model MIROC (Model for Interdisciplinary 
Research on Climate) which has been cooperatively developed by the 
University of Tokyo, NIES, and JAMSTEC (K-1 model developers, 2004; 
Nozawa et al., 2007) 
NorESM1-M (Norwegian Earth System Model) based on the Community Climate 
System Model version 4 (CCSM4) of the University Corporation for 
Atmospheric Research. 
 
The selected Representative Carbon Pathways (RCP) applied to the models are: 
RCP Description 
rcp2p6 The emission pathway is representative for scenarios in the literature 
leading to very low greenhouse gas concentration levels. It is a so-called 
"peak" scenario: represents a strong mitigation scenario and is 
extended by assuming constant emissions after 2100 (including net 
negative CO2 emissions), leading to CO2 concentrations returning to 
360 ppm by 2300. 
rcp4p5 It is a stabilization scenario where total radiative forcing is stabilized 
before 2100 by employment of a range of technologies and strategies 
for reducing greenhouse gas emissions. 
rcp6p0 It is a stabilization scenario where total radiative forcing is stabilized 
after 2100 without overshoot by employment of a range of technologies 
and strategies for reducing greenhouse gas emissions. 
rcp8p5 It is characterized by increasing greenhouse gas emissions over time 
representative for scenarios in the literature leading to high greenhouse 
gas concentration levels. The underlying scenario drivers and resulting 
development path are based on the A2r scenario detailed in Riahi et al. 
(2007).

Each raster filename follows a structured naming convention that encodes key information about 
the data. The format is: <res01 variable>_<source climate dataset or climate 
model>_<emission scenario or pathway code>_<time period>.tif. This includes: 
• the res01 variable (e.g., “et0” for reference evapotranspiration, see GAEZ v4 variable 
listing in Annex I) 
• the climate model (e.g., GFDL-ESM4) 
• the scenario (e.g., rcp2p6, rcp4p5, rcp6p0, rcp5p85 or hist that is used for historical 
data modelled using CRUTS32 climate data) 
• the time period 
For example, historical datasets use paths such as 
\res01\CRUTS32\Hist\cbt_CRUTS32_Hist_0010.tif, while future projections follow formats like 
\res01\GFDL-ESM2M\rcp2p6\cbt_GFDL-ESM2M_rcp2p6_2020s.tif.

Data structure and naming convention for crop summary tables 
In the summary tables folder of the GAEZ v4 data repository are presented the tabulated results of 
the crop evaluation in Module V (res05) on suitability and attainable yield. 
The results have been aggregated and tabulated by administrative units (country or 
country/province for some major countries, and by sub-continental and continental regional 
aggregations) and by broad hydro-regions. The crop summary tables are organized by land cover 
class, protection/exclusion class and AEZ class and summarize by suitability class the suitable 
extents, attainable production and yields, various constraint factors (due to thermal regime, 
moisture deficits, agro-climatic constraints due to pest, disease and workability limitations, and due 
to soil/terrain limitations) and aggregate simulated water deficits (rain-fed conditions) 
respectively net irrigation requirements (irrigated conditions). 
Crop summary tables are provided for 53 crops, separately for rain-fed and irrigated conditions, 
and for historical and future climate scenarios. For 12 major countries, summary statistic were 
prepared also at sub-national level. Those countries have been selected based on their ranking in 
terms of three criteria: (i) total country area, (ii) total cropland area, and (iii) average size of sub-
national administrative units. 
The data are organized in three folders for historical data (CRUTS32), projected data for ENSEMBLE 
mean climate scenario and for the single five climate scenarios. Users can download the data by 
crop, geographic level, climate data source, and input level. The result is downloadable as .zip file 
containing the crop summary tables in .csv format.

Annex II – Import symbology in desktop GIS Software 
Import symbology in ArcGIS Pro 
When loaded in desktop GIS softwares, categorical data are visualized with random colors for each 
class. To visualize in ArcGIS Pro the categorical GAEZ v4 data with the correct symbology, load the 
data in the Map, right-click on the layer name and select the Symbology menu. 
 
This will open the Symbology menu, click on the three lines button on the top right corner and 
select the Import Colormap option. 
Select the corresponding .clr file depending on the GAEZ v4 data that has been downloaded and 
added in ArcGIS Pro. Information about the corresponding color map file to use are given in Annex 
II of this User’s Guide. Click OK. The Colormap will be adeed to the layer. This will allow the user to 
display the layer with the pre-defined set of colors for each class. 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 

 
It is suggested to use the same styles as in the GAEZ v4 Data Portal also for continuous data. Colors 
ramps to use for continuous GAEZ v4 data are stored in the ArcGIS Pro style file (GAEZv4 
Styles.stylx). To add a style to an ArcGIS project, follow these steps: 
• On the Insert tab, in the Styles group, click Add  and then Add Style; 
• Browse to the location of the style 
• Click OK to add the style to the project. 
Load the data downloaded from the GAEZv4 Data Portal in the Map, right-click on the layer name 
and select the Symbology menu. The available color schemes will be in the drop-down list of the 
Symbology panel. Ensure that a Stretch Symbology is applied with Minimum Maximum Stretch 
type. 
 

Select the corresponding color ramp from the drop down list (i.e. in the example below, with the 
Mean annual temperature (°C) layer, the corresponding color ramp to adopt is 
AgroClimaticDaysColorRamp, see Annex II). Now the layer is visualized with the correct color 
scheme. 
 
Import symbology in QGIS 
After having downloaded the layer from the GAEZ v4 platform, open the .TIF file in QGIS. 
 
Categorical data are visualized with random colors for each class. To visualize the layer with the 
correct symbology, open the layer properties window by double clicking on the layer in the Layers 
panel and go to the Symbology tab. 
For categorical data ensure to choose the Paletted/Unique values as Renderer type. Click on the 
three dots button (red circle in screenshot below) and use the option Load colour map from file to 

load the corresponding .clr file that can be downloaded from the GAEZ v4 platform (i.e. in the 
example below, with the Thermal Zones layer select the ThermalZones.clr file, see Annex II). Click 
Apply. 
 
Once loaded, the layer will be visualized with the pre-defined set of colors for each class and with 
the corresponding class name. 
 
 

 
For continuous data it is suggested to use the same styles as in the GAEZ v4 Data Portal.  
Color ramps have been saved as .xml files. To add color ramps in QGIS, load the .xml files from the 
Settings – Style Manager menu, click on the Import button and search for the GAEZ .xml files. 
 
 
Select the .xml file, select the “Add to favourites” option and the item to import. Click on Import. 
After having opened the .TIF file in QGIS, open the layer properties window by double clicking on 
the layer in the Layers panel and go to the Symbology tab. Choose Singleband pseudocolor as 
Render type and select the corresponding color ramp from the drop down list (i.e. in the example 
below, with the Total number of growing period days layer the color ramp to use is the 
AgroClimaticPrecipitation_ColorRamp.xml, see Annex II). Click on Apply.
