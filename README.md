## Overview

Environmental degradation frequently arises from the trade-off between boosting agricultural productivity and overexploiting natural resources. Traditional institutional responses — Pigouvian taxes, Coasian bargaining, regulatory enforcement — address these externalities only partially, often failing to account for long-term ecological uncertainty and endogenous agent behavior.

This thesis introduces the concept of  **economic commensalism** : a framework that repositions private incentives to align with collective environmental goals, making preservation not a legal obligation, but a  **individually rational and collectively optimal strategy** .

Rather than treating the environment as an exogenous constraint, economic commensalism endogenizes it as a **productive factor (E)** that amplifies the returns to capital (K) and labor (L) — shifting the equilibrium from extraction to conservation through well-calibrated market-based mechanisms.

---

## Research Problem

> *Is it possible to effectively and efficiently reconcile private productivity and profitability with socially desirable environmental preservation through well-structured economic incentives, surpassing the economic performance of the current extractivism system?*

---

## General Hypothesis (H0)

> *It is possible to design an economic-institutional framework grounded in incentive-compatible practices that jointly optimize environmental performance and private productivity and profitability, thereby overcoming the theoretical and practical limitations of externality-based models.*

### Specific Hypotheses

| #            | Hypothesis                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| ------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **H1** | Incorporating the environment as an endogenous productive factor generates higher long-run output than extractivist models                                                                               |
| **H2** | Intertemporal optimization under ecological feedback leads agents to adopt conservation strategies without coercion                                                                                      |
| **H3** | Territorial and biome-specific incentive structures outperform uniform regulatory approaches                                                                                                             |
| **H4** | Well-calibrated economic incentives induce voluntary internalization of preservation, reducing reliance on punitive measures                                                                             |
| **H5** | Under ecological feedback, bounded rationality, and calibrated incentives, agent interactions tend toward a stable Nash equilibrium where preservation is individually rational and collectively optimal |

---

## Extractivism vs. Economic Commensalism

| Dimension                               | Extractivism (current paradigm)                                                      | Economic Commensalism (proposed)                                                                        |
| --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Relationship with environment** | Natural resource as exogenous stock, used up to regeneration limit                   | Environment as **endogenous productive factor (E)** , strategic asset that amplifies K and L    |
| **Dominant incentives**           | State coercion, fines, taxes, diffuse subsidies                                      | **Positive, calibrated, territorial** incentives tied to local environmental performance         |
| **Agent rationality**             | Short-term profit maximizer; environment as external barrier                         | **Intertemporal maximizer** : preservation becomes rational because it generates economic returns |
| **Institutional structure**       | Centralized vertical regulation (Código Florestal, embargos), often poorly enforced | Decentralized, adaptive, bottom-up governance anchored in verifiable environmental data                 |
| **Long-run economic outcome**     | Declining marginal productivity, rising costs (pests, soil degradation)              | **Rising marginal productivity** of K and L via preservation of factor E                         |
| **Expected equilibrium**          | Unstable; tends to breach tipping points and generate crises                         | Stable strategic equilibrium:**preservation is individually rational and collectively optimal**   |
| **Role of the state**             | Enforcer and punisher                                                                | **Value inducer**: structures incentives, verifies performance                                    |

## Thesis Structure

The research is organized into  **three complementary chapters** :

### Chapter 1 — Theoretical Framework

Develops the ontological and epistemological foundations of economic commensalism. Introduces a typology for ecological-economic strategies and critiques the shortcomings of orthodox externality-based models. Methods: intertemporal optimization, uncertainty management, and dynamic learning processes.

### Chapter 2 — Mathematical Modeling

Formalizes the proposed mechanisms using Bellman equations, game theory, and adaptive learning models. Assesses the internal coherence and logical outcomes of the commensalism framework. Tests whether incentive-compatible equilibria exist and are stable.

### Chapter 3 — Empirical Validation

Applies econometric and geospatial analysis using real-world data from  **Mato Grosso** , Brazil — a region at the intersection of the Amazon, Cerrado, and Pantanal biomes, facing escalating deforestation and agricultural expansion. Tests whether the theoretical model's predictions hold in practice.

---

## Empirical Context: Mato Grosso, Brazil

Mato Grosso serves as the primary empirical field for this research due to its unique position:

* Heart of Brazil's agricultural frontier (soy, cattle, cotton)
* Home to three major biomes: **Amazon, Cerrado, and Pantanal**
* Active carbon and land markets with significant policy variation
* Ongoing tension between agribusiness productivity and conservation obligations

The region offers ideal conditions to test whether rational eco-economic dynamics can harmonize productivity with sustainable conservation without relying solely on coercion.

## Contributions

### Theoretical

* Introduces economic commensalism as a formal alternative to externality-based paradigms
* Endogenizes the environment within production theory
* Provides a unified framework integrating dynamic optimization, game theory, and ecological feedback

### Practical

* Designs incentive-compatible policy mechanisms applicable to tropical agricultural frontiers
* Offers a market-driven governance model scalable to other regions facing similar trade-offs
* Informs the design of carbon markets, environmental credit systems, and conservation subsidies
